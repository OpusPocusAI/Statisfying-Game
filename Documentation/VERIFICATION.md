## Performance observations

On the supplied AMD Ryzen 5 7500F / Radeon RX 7600 XT machine, the latest ocean-polish 1080p offscreen sample averaged 11.12 ms (p95 11.11 ms) across 300 restored-studio frames. This does not benchmark truck driving. The first mosaic-polish 1080p standalone offscreen sample averaged 42.04 ms across 300 restored-studio frames, with a 139.73 ms 95th percentile. Earlier samples averaged roughly 11.1 to 19.3 ms. A read-only process check found another game and recording software running concurrently; this makes the run unsuitable for attributing the slowdown to these changes. Those applications were left alone. A repeat passed functionally and averaged 20.36 ms (p95 57.80 ms); that repeat was the previous delivery report. These runs do not establish acceptable foreground performance or rule out a game-side regression. These runs were not controlled comparisons and do not establish the cause of the variation or prove a performance improvement from gameplay fixes. The game targets 90 frames per second; this hidden-player check is not a presented-window FPS benchmark, worst-case profile, or low-spec guarantee. A live Editor sample reported approximately 17 ms CPU and 4.2 ms GPU; another sample was slower. The studio view reported about 18,333 triangles, 260 batches and 526 draw calls. A foreground performance pass is still needed before claiming consistent smoothness.

## Remaining acceptance work and limitations

- A normal human playthrough must measure the 30–45-minute target and the first-upgrade 5–8-minute target. Automated accelerated journeys cannot validate them.
- No comprehensive human usability or boundary sweep has been completed. Recovery paths have automated coverage, but that is not proof against every possible trapping position.
- Original procedural audio remains deliberately simple. Listening, mixing and perceived satisfaction need a human pass.
- Geometry remains visibly economical: the truck now has bevelled bodywork, wheel arches/hubs, a grille, lights and clearer cargo rails, but its cab, static steering wheel, decorative mirrors and representative cargo remain simple. Gathering tools now have shaped geometry and usable open containers, but contents remain a neutral representative pile. Shoreline parasols, benches, dunes, grass, dig piles and rubbish now have a dedicated geometry pass. Nine new offshore rocks give the coves pale limestone, low tidal shelves and taller sandstone silhouettes; canopy fabric has a closed profile, softer hems and visible ribs. Inland props still repeat, and richer surface treatment remains worthwhile. Broken contact foam and outward wash now follow the nine offshore rocks, using a combined mesh and animated shader; the ocean now uses warped brush marks, broken shore crests, filtered distant detail and a restrained sun sheen. It remains a flat opaque stylized surface. The sand-to-water edge now uses a blended shoulder with a dry-to-wet wash instead of a creased grey strip; larger-scale shoreline shaping remains worthwhile. The studio now has pale ceiling lining, folded curtains, shaped pendants, ceramics and linen storage; these are static stylized details. The coastal palette, glass transformation and illuminated triptych are implemented; richer surfaces and less repetitive composition remain worthwhile.
- Lighting uses a fixed golden-hour setup, modest point lights and authored floor-light shapes with feathered edges and warm per-panel gallery lamps. The floor shapes are a stylized effect, not simulated optical projection.

The highest-value next work is a timed usability playthrough, stronger prop/shoreline composition, and richer layered audio. Keep that work inside the compact scope; do not add open-world systems, weather, NPCs or complex vehicle simulation.
